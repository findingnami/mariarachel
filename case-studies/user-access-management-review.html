Case Study

User Access Management Review

Reviewing user roles, permissions, and access removal across internal commission, project management, and CRM applications, and building safeguards to keep access appropriate.

Focus
Logical access
Context
Three internal business applications
Tools
Supabase, Vercel
Role
Operations Intelligence Manager

Overview

At a technology advisory firm, I review who has access to what across three internal business applications: commission tracking, project management, and CRM. The aim is to make sure access matches each person's role and is removed when it is no longer needed.

Risk & objective

Internal applications hold contract, commission, and client information. Excess permissions, or accounts that stay active after someone changes role or leaves, create the risk of unauthorized viewing or changes, and make it harder to trust the records. The objective is to keep access limited to what each role needs, to catch access that should be removed, and to keep a trail of what users do.

Approach

  1. Define the scope. List the applications in the review: commission tracking, project management, and CRM.
  2. List users and roles. Record who has an account in each application and which role they hold.
  3. Compare access to need. Check that each role's permissions match what that role needs to do its job.
  4. Identify access to change. Flag accounts or permissions that are no longer needed.
  5. Remove or adjust, then confirm. Apply the change and check that the access is actually gone.
  6. Build in safeguards. Set up role-based access, data restrictions, two-factor authentication, and activity logs so that access stays appropriate between reviews.

Result

Access across the three applications is governed by defined roles, with safeguards built into the applications instead of relying on manual checks alone, and with logs that record user actions.

Through an audit lens

This is the control area that IT general controls testing calls logical access: provisioning, periodic access review, removal of leavers, authentication, and logging. I have worked on both sides of it, designing the controls and checking that they operate, which is the view an auditor needs when deciding what evidence to request and how to test it.
